## What genuine compound steam would change

Native B99 mean torque assumes a fixed1bar atmospheric backpressure. Wires alone
cannot make HP work against a receiver. Future true compound operation needs an
owned per-car stage calculation, preserving native crank timing, lubrication,
condensate/damage and saved state. Stage ownership must follow actual geometry:
MainEngine=FRONT LP, RearEngine=REAR HP, MiddleEngine=Erie TENDER LP. Earlier
prototype notes calling the main engine rear HP were incorrect and are corrected.

At the same pressure/cutoff/RPM, HP backpressure reduces HP work, and LP inlet
pressure comes from the receiver rather than another boiler feed. Starting pull
and wheel-rim torque therefore change; a cold empty receiver delays LP assistance.
A deliberate starting-valve design may be needed, rather than free LP steam.
Fresh boiler demand becomes HP intake plus any actual direct starting supply;
LP transfer is internal and must not be charged again. Regulator response, water
use, boiler-pressure recovery and sustained power consequently change together.
Only atmospheric exhaust feeds the chimney: HP transfer is internal; Erie tender
LP exhaust stays at the tender. This also changes locomotive exhaust draught and
can affect firing/steaming. It cannot be treated as a harmless sound-only change.

These are steam-drive changes, not permission to retune adhesion, mass, wheel
radius, curving, sanding or brakes. Existing grip settings can remain, but slip
onset still changes when delivered torque changes. Before enabling compounds,
compare actual existing calibrated bores/chest volumes/flow limits, chosen
receiver volumes and independent speeds; establish starting behaviour and native
save/pooling restoration. No promises of extra power or quantified economy yet.
